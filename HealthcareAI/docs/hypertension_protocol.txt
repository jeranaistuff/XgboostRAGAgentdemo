Document: Hypertension Diagnosis and Treatment Protocol v2.1
Last Updated: February 2025

BLOOD PRESSURE CLASSIFICATION (Riverside MC adopts AHA 2024 guidelines)
- Normal          : Systolic < 120 AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Elevated        : Systolic 120-129 AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 HTN     : Systolic 130-139 OR Diastolic 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 HTN     : Systolic >= 140 OR Diastolic >= 90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: Systolic > 180 AND/OR Diastolic > 120 mmHg (immediate referral)

DIAGNOSIS REQUIREMENTS
- Confirm with 2 readings at 2 separate visits, at least 1 week apart
- Home blood pressure monitoring recommended: average > 130/80 confirms diagnosis
- White coat hypertension: office BP elevated but home BP normal — treat lifestyle only
- Masked hypertension: office BP normal but home BP elevated — treat as hypertension

TREATMENT TARGETS
- General population: < 130/80 mmHg
- Age > 80 years: < 150/90 mmHg (to avoid orthostatic hypotension risk)
- Diabetes patients: < 130/80 mmHg
- CKD with proteinuria: < 130/80 mmHg

FIRST-LINE MEDICATIONS
1. Thiazide diuretics: chlorthalidone 12.5-25mg daily (preferred over HCTZ at Riverside MC)
2. ACE inhibitors: lisinopril 10-40mg daily (avoid in pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis)
3. ARBs: losartan 50-100mg daily (use if ACE inhibitor causes cough)
4. Calcium channel blockers: amlodipine 5-10mg daily (good for elderly, isolated systolic HTN)

COMBINATION THERAPY
- If BP > 20/10 mmHg above goal, start with two drugs simultaneously
- Preferred combo: ACE inhibitor/ARB + CCB or thiazide
- Avoid combining ACE inhibitor + ARB (increased adverse effects, no added benefit)

LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS (required regardless of medication)
- DASH diet: reduces BP by 8-14 mmHg
- Sodium restriction: < 2.3g/day (ideally < 1.5g/day)
- Weight loss: each 1kg lost reduces BP by ~1 mmHg
- Exercise: 90-150 min/week aerobic activity
- Limit alcohol: < 1 drink/day women, < 2 drinks/day men
- Smoking cessation (does not directly lower BP but critical for CV risk reduction)

RESISTANT HYPERTENSION
Defined as BP above goal despite 3 optimal-dose antihypertensives (including a diuretic).
- Rule out secondary causes: renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, sleep apnea
- Add spironolactone 25-50mg (strong evidence for resistant HTN)
- Refer to hypertension specialist at Riverside MC Cardiology Dept (ext. 4421)

HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCY vs URGENCY
- Emergency (end-organ damage present): ICU admission, IV labetalol or nicardipine
  Target: reduce MAP by no more than 25% in first hour
- Urgency (no end-organ damage): oral medications, reduce BP over 24-48 hours
